# Reproducing Mikkelsen et al. (2017) with a different modelling algorithm

An attempt to reproduce a published multi-site MRS study by re-analysing the
**same raw spectra** with **Osprey** instead of **Gannet**, changing the fitting
software and nothing else.

**Status:** analysis complete for the primary comparison; one sensitivity
analysis incomplete (see Limitations). All figures below are regenerated by
`analysis/analyse.R` from the files in `results/`.

---

## The question

GABA cannot be measured directly. A scanner produces a noisy spectrum, and the
reported concentration is whatever number falls out when a model is fitted to
it. Every published GABA value is therefore partly a fact about the brain and
partly a fact about the software that fitted the curve — and papers almost never
separate the two.

[Big GABA](https://www.nitrc.org/projects/biggaba/) is an unusually good place
to try. The acquisition is tightly controlled across sites: identical sequence
(MEGA-PRESS, TR 2000 ms, TE 68 ms, 320 averages), identical voxel (medial
parietal, 30x30x30 mm), three scanner vendors.
[Mikkelsen et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2017.07.021)
analysed 272 participants across 24 sites in **Gannet** and reported GABA+/Cr of
0.116 +/- 0.014, a whole-dataset CV of 12.0%, and variance partitioning 72%
subject / 20% site / 8% vendor.

This project re-runs those spectra through **Osprey** and asks what survives.

### A prediction registered in advance

This was not open-ended. [Craven et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1002/nbm.4702)
compared seven algorithms on this repository and found the **algorithm accounted
for 33.8% of variance against 4.0% for scanner vendor**, with an ICC across tools
of 0.38. They specifically reported Osprey giving **~28% lower** estimates on
Siemens data.

That 28% figure was written into `docs/target-values.md` **before any data was
processed**, as a falsifiable expectation.

## Findings

### 1. Within-site precision reproduces; between-site spread roughly doubles

| | this reproduction | published (Gannet) |
|---|---|---|
| mean within-site CV | **11.1%** | 9.5% |
| whole-dataset CV | **20.6%** (17.3% excl. P3) | 12.0% |

Osprey is about as precise as Gannet *within* a site, while spreading sites and
vendors much further apart. That is the signature of a systematic algorithm
effect rather than added noise, and it is consistent with Craven et al.'s
variance partition.

### 2. The anomaly is confined to Siemens

| vendor | sites | n | GABA+/tCr | between-site CV | GABA collapsed to ~0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GE | 6 | 67 | 0.3764 | 6.2% | 2/67 (3%) |
| Philips | 8 | 89 | 0.3688 | 18.4% | 0/89 (0%) |
| **Siemens** | 3 | 36 | **0.2703** | **2.8%** | **28/36 (78%)** |

Vendor ratios (scale-invariant, so valid despite the unreconciled absolute
scale — see below):

| pair | observed | published |
|---|---|---|
| GE / Philips | 1.021 (**1.089** excl. P3) | 1.108 |
| GE / Siemens | **1.392** | 1.060 |
| Siemens / Philips | **0.733** | 1.045 |

**GE and Philips reproduce the published relationship** — 1.089 against 1.108
once the unexplained P3 outlier is set aside. This acts as a control: the
pipeline is not generically inflating vendor differences.

**Siemens does not.** Against the mean of the other two vendors:

```
observed   0.726          published   0.991      ->  Siemens runs 26.8% low
excl. P3   0.749          published   0.991      ->  Siemens runs 24.4% low
```

Craven et al. predicted **~28%**. The three Siemens sites agree with one another
to within a between-site CV of **2.8%** — tighter than GE's six sites — so this
is not one anomalous site.

### 3. A visible candidate mechanism

Osprey models GABA and the co-edited macromolecule separately, then reports
their sum as `GABAplus`. On Siemens data that partition collapses: **78% of
Siemens subjects** have the GABA component driven to approximately zero with the
macromolecule absorbing the signal, against 3% for GE and 0% for Philips.

The sum stays well determined while its components do not — the signature of
collinearity between two basis functions that overlap at 3.0 ppm. At 3T these
are not separable, which is precisely why the literature reports "GABA+" rather
than GABA.

Two Siemens-specific phenomena co-occurring this cleanly across 17 sites are
unlikely to be independent, but the causal link is **not established**. It is the
most valuable open question here.

## What this does not show

**The absolute scale is not reconciled.** Osprey's `GABAplus` (GABA basis
function + MM09) and Gannet's GABA+ (a three-Gaussian fit to the whole 3.0 ppm
peak, creatine-referenced with its own proton scaling) are differently
constructed quantities. Raw values differ by roughly 2.7x and **must not be
compared directly**. Every comparison above is a ratio or a CV, both
scale-invariant. See `docs/discrepancy-log.md` D-08.

**The fit ranges differ.** Osprey ran at its default 0.2-4.2 ppm; Gannet used
2.79-4.10 ppm. A sensitivity analysis re-running everything at Gannet's range is
**incomplete** — 11 of 17 sites, with all three Siemens sites lost to memory
errors — so it cannot yet address the headline comparison. This is the largest
open confound. See D-07.

**P3 is unexplained.** One Philips site sits 5.2 SD above its peers. Four
hypotheses were tested and all rejected: residual water (the correlation is
entirely P3 itself, r = +0.06 across the other 77 Philips subjects), scanner
software generation (P6 shares it and is among the lowest), outlier subjects
(all 12 elevated uniformly), and protocol differences (SPAR headers match). P3
is **retained**; all headline figures are reported with and without it. See D-17.

**The published sample no longer exists in full.** See below.

## Data availability is itself a finding

The public MEGA-PRESS release contains **20 of the 24 sites** analysed in 2017.
Six are absent entirely (G2, G3, P2, S2, S4, S7); two present sites postdate the
paper (P10, S8) and are out of scope. A further site, **S3, cannot be read by
Osprey at all** — it is Siemens VD software with a multi-RAID TWIX container, and
Osprey's bundled `mapVBVD` fails in `read_twix_hdr.m:15` before any processing,
with an error message that gives no hint of the cause.

| | published (2017) | achievable here |
|---|---|---|
| sites | 24 | **17** |
| subjects | 272 | **192** |
| GE / Philips / Siemens | 91 / 104 / 77 | 67 / 89 / 36 |

The exact published sample **cannot be reconstructed**. Siemens is hit hardest,
falling from 7 sites to 3 — the vendor arm carrying the main finding.

## The analysis tool required modification

Running this pipeline required patching Osprey and discovering undocumented
requirements. Both are documented and reproducible:

- **49 lines across 21 files** apply the colon operator to `MRSCont.nDatasets`,
  which is a 1x2 vector. Historically a MATLAB *warning* that silently used the
  first element (the correct value); recent MATLAB makes it a hard error, so the
  latent bug becomes fatal on upgrade. Patched via
  `patches/apply_osprey_patches.py` (idempotent, dry-run capable, emits a
  manifest). Reported upstream.
- Osprey's documentation lists **two** required toolboxes; the true count is at
  least **three** (Signal Processing is undocumented), plus a File Exchange
  package for figure export. `matlab/check_toolboxes.m` performs a static
  dependency scan to produce the real list.

**This analysis therefore runs a modified Osprey.** The patch touches no signal
processing or fitting mathematics — see `patches/` for the full diff and
rationale.

## Repository layout

```
analysis/analyse.R          regenerates every reported number from results/
matlab/
  check_environment.m       MATLAB version, toolboxes, Osprey, SPM, data
  check_toolboxes.m         static dependency scan for real requirements
  smoke_test.m              one dataset end to end - run this first
  run_osprey_batch.m        all sites; RUN_LABEL / FIT_RANGE configurable
  qc_plot.m                 fit inspection without the GUI Layout Toolbox
  writeOspreyJobFile.m      generates Osprey job files (committed per run)
docs/
  target-values.md          every published number being reproduced, sourced
  analysis-decisions.md     each judgment call, marked LOCKED / OPEN / FORCED
  discrepancy-log.md        19 entries - the substance of the project
patches/                    Osprey modifications + manifest
results/                    per-site outputs; tables/ from analyse.R
```

`docs/discrepancy-log.md` is the centre of this project. It records what
diverged, what was investigated, what was ruled out, and which hypotheses failed
— including the ones that looked right at first.

## Reproducing this

1. **Data.** Download the MEGA-PRESS packages from
   [NITRC](https://www.nitrc.org/projects/biggaba/) into `data/` — in-scope
   sites only (see `data/README.md`), plus `demographics.csv` and
   `voxel_tissue_fractions.csv`. Roughly 3 GB compressed, ~55 GB unpacked.
2. **Osprey.** Clone it, then apply the patches:
   `python3 patches/apply_osprey_patches.py <path-to-osprey>`
   (use `--check` first to see what would change).
3. **Environment.** `check_environment` in MATLAB, then `check_toolboxes`.
4. **Smoke test.** `smoke_test` — one dataset, then look at the fit with
   `qc_plot`. Do not proceed on a plausible number alone.
5. **Batch.** `run_osprey_batch` — about 5 hours for 17 sites, resumable.
6. **Analysis.** `Rscript analysis/analyse.R`.
